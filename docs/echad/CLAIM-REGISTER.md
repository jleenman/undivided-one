## C-001 — `echad` versus `yachid`

- Current formulation: “Echad betekent niet ‘één in getal’ (dat zou jachid zijn), maar één in ondeelbare trouw en identiteit”; later: “echad functioneert hier niet numeriek, maar identitair.”
- Live/local: `/nl/essay`, `/en/essay`; `content/nl/essay.md`, `content/en/essay.md`. Related confession sources: `content/{nl,en}/confession.md`.
- Type: lexical, grammatical, exegetical.
- Explicit observations: `אֶחָד` is the ordinary numeral/adjective “one” and is frequently numerical; `יָחִיד` means only/only one/solitary; Deut 6:4 reads tersely `יהוה אלהינו יהוה אחד` without an expressed present-tense copula.
- Not explicit: neither word lexically encodes a doctrine of God's internal composition; Deut 6:4 does not contrast `echad` with `yachid`.
- Interpretive step: the live essay turns a theological identity model into the lexical meaning of `echad`.
- Strongest alternative: “JHWH is one” predicates unity/uniqueness; “JHWH alone” demands exclusive covenant loyalty. Both resist rival gods without defining later ontological categories.
- Primary sources: Deut 6:4–9; Gen 1:5; Gen 22:2, 12, 16; Zech 14:9; Mark 12:29–34.
- Secondary sources: BDB entries 259/3173; Tigay 1996, 76–79, 438–44; Janzen 1987; Block 2004; NRSVue/JPS translation notes.
- Confidence: `hoog` that the dichotomy is false; `middel` on the best single translation of Deut 6:4.
- Risk: high; a demonstrably weak lexical pillar undermines the wider thesis.
- Recommended action: replace with bounded wording in P-001–P-004.
- Task/status: ECHAD-002; `review`.

- Current formulation: “geen representatieve uitspraak ... maar een identitaire”; “De tekst laat geen ruimte voor een ontologische tussenlaag of een verborgen ‘ander’.”
- Live/local: `/nl/essay`, `/en/essay`; `content/{nl,en}/essay.md`.
- Type: exegetical, systematic.
- Explicit observations: Jesus answers Philip with “who has seen me has seen the Father,” followed by mutual indwelling language and distinction between Jesus' words and the Father dwelling/working in him.
- Not explicit: the verse does not use modern “numerical identity,” “same subject,” or “mere representation” terminology.

- Strongest alternative: a classical trinitarian reading sees perfect revelation grounded in unity while preserving Father/Son distinction; a unitarian agency reading treats Jesus as the unique authorized revealer.
- Primary sources: John 14:7–11; John 1:18; 5; 17.
- Secondary sources: pending task review.
- Confidence: `middel` that current exclusivity overstates the verse.
- Risk/action/task/status: high; qualify through ECHAD-004 and ECHAD-009; `unreviewed`.
