# ECHAD-002 — Sjemá, `echad` en `yachid`

- Date: 2026-07-12
- Status: `review`
- Live routes consulted: `/nl/essay`, `/en/essay`, `/nl/argument`, `/en/argument`, `/nl/confession`, `/en/confession`
- Canonical changes: none; proposals only

## Claim under review

The live essay says: “Echad betekent niet ‘één in getal’ (dat zou jachid zijn), maar één in ondeelbare trouw en identiteit,” and later that `echad` functions “not numerically but as an identity category.”

## Explicit observations

1. The consonantal/vocalized Masoretic wording of Deut 6:4 is `שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ יְהוָה אֶחָד`—literally terse nominal syntax: “Hear, Israel, YHWH our-God YHWH one.” Biblical Hebrew supplies no expressed present-tense copula here.
2. `אֶחָד` (`ʾeḥad`) is the ordinary cardinal/adjectival “one.” It is plainly numerical in passages such as Gen 1:5 (“one day/day one”) and throughout counting/ordinal-like usage. Context can yield “one,” “single,” “same,” “each,” or in rare constructions an exclusivity sense; it does not lexically mean “indivisible identity rather than numerical one.”
3. `יָחִיד` (`yāḥîd`) means “only/only one, unique, solitary,” used especially of an only or uniquely beloved child (Gen 22:2, 12, 16; Judg 11:34) and solitary persons/life. It is not the standard alternative cardinal numeral the author would have had to use for numerical oneness.
4. Deut 6:4's syntax admits multiple established renderings: “YHWH our God, YHWH is one”; “YHWH is our God, YHWH alone”; and “YHWH our God is one YHWH.” NRSVue and JPS foreground “YHWH/LORD alone” while documenting “is one” alternatives; older and many Christian translations foreground “is one.”
5. The immediate context (Deut 6:5–15) commands undivided love, remembrance, obedience, and refusal of other gods. This strongly supports exclusive covenant allegiance as a central pragmatic force, whether `echad` is rendered “one” or “alone.”

## What is not explicit

- The verse does not contrast `echad` with `yachid`.
- Neither lexeme by itself states “compound unity,” “one person,” “one relational subject,” “one essence in three persons,” or the Echad model's “one multidimensional identity.”
- Deut 6:4 does not present an analysis of God's inner structure. It identifies YHWH as Israel's God and demands/declares unrivalled oneness and allegiance.
- The semantic value of a word cannot by itself decide later trinitarian, unitarian, modalist, or identity-model ontology.

## Historical and literary frame

Deut 6:4 opens a covenant exhortation following the Decalogue recap. The singular address to Israel and ensuing commands orient the confession toward exclusive love and transmission. Later Jewish reception makes the Sjemá a central monotheistic confession; Mark 12:29–34 joins it to the love command without explaining divine internal structure.

## Strongest alternative readings

- **Exclusive-loyalty reading:** “YHWH is our God, YHWH alone.” This best foregrounds Deuteronomy's covenant polemic, though `levad` is the more ordinary word for “alone” and therefore the exact lexical/syntactic case is debated.
- **Unity/uniqueness reading:** “YHWH our God, YHWH is one.” This predicates oneness/uniqueness and fits later reception, but still does not specify whether or how distinctions could exist within God's identity.
- **Classical trinitarian steelman:** the Sjemá excludes polytheism and confesses one God; trinitarianism claims to preserve rather than divide that numerical divine unity while grounding personal distinctions elsewhere. The word `echad` neither proves nor disproves this model.
- **Unitarian/Oneness steelman:** the natural force of one named YHWH weighs against multiplying divine centers/subjects; nonetheless, moving from that observation to a complete person/subject ontology requires additional texts and definitions.

## Evidence weighting

The lexical correction is decisive: the public `echad`/`yachid` dichotomy is false. The exact best translation of Deut 6:4 is less certain because the nominal syntax is compressed and its pragmatic focus can combine oneness with exclusivity. The immediate context makes exclusive loyalty highly salient. None of these points authorizes turning “identity-based, multidimensional revelation” into the dictionary meaning of `echad`.

## Provisional conclusion

Deut 6:4 is a foundational confession of YHWH's unrivalled oneness and Israel's exclusive allegiance. `Echad` can be numerically “one”; `yachid` is “only/unique/solitary,” not the required word for numerical one. The verse constrains every Christian model to reject rival gods and divided worship, but alone does not map God's inner structure. Confidence: **hoog** on the lexical correction; **middel** on choosing one English/Dutch rendering as exhaustive.

## Revision criteria

Revise this conclusion if a standard lexicon/grammar or primary corpus evidence establishes that `echad` cannot function numerically, that `yachid` is the normal cardinal numeral in the relevant syntax, or that Deut 6:4 grammatically excludes all but one translation. Current evidence points the other way.
